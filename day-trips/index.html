Day trips from Barcelona

Beyond the city — mountains, medieval towns, Roman ruins and Costa Brava beaches, all reachable by public transport. Each place below shows exactly which train or bus to take, and whether you need to change along the way.

Year-Round

Worth visiting whatever the season — mountains, medieval streets, Roman ruins and museums that don't depend on the weather.

Mountain & monastery

Montserrat

A jagged mountain silhouette an hour from the city, home to the Benedictine monastery of Santa Maria de Montserrat and the Black Madonna. Hiking trails, panoramic viewpoints and a boys' choir performance on weekday afternoons.

How to get there

Train: FGC line R5 from Plaça Espanya to Monistrol de Montserrat (or Montserrat-Aeri), then the Cremallera rack railway or the Aeri cable car up the mountain.

Change needed — swap from the train to the rack railway or cable car at the foot of the mountain.

Bus: No regular public bus line; the train + rack railway route above is the standard way in.

Medieval city

Girona

Colourful houses lining the Onyar river, one of Europe's widest Gothic cathedral naves, a walkable Jewish Quarter, and several Game of Thrones filming locations tucked into the old stone streets.

How to get there

Train: Direct Rodalies R11 or high-speed AVE/Avant from Barcelona-Sants or Passeig de Gràcia to Girona.

No change needed — direct service, roughly 40 minutes to 1h20 depending on the train.

Bus: Direct coach from Barcelona Estació del Nord, about 1h15.

Roman ruins

Tarragona

The former capital of Roman Hispania, with a UNESCO-listed amphitheatre overlooking the Mediterranean, a Roman circus, city walls, and a lively seafront promenade for afterwards.

How to get there

Train: Direct Rodalies R16 from Barcelona-Sants (~1h15), or high-speed AVE (~35 min).

No change needed — direct service either way.

Bus: Direct coach from Barcelona Estació del Nord, about 1h30.

Museum

Figueres — Dalí Museum

Salvador Dalí's own surrealist Theatre-Museum, built inside Figueres' former municipal theatre and topped with giant eggs — the artist is buried in the crypt below. A good rainy-day option since it's entirely indoors.

How to get there

Train: Direct Renfe service (AVE/Avant/regional R11) from Barcelona-Sants to Figueres or Figueres-Vilafant.

No change needed — journey time ranges from about 1 hour (fast train) to 2 hours (regional).

Bus: Direct coach from Barcelona Estació del Nord, about 2h45.

Medieval village

Besalú

A tiny fortified village crossed by a dramatic Romanesque bridge, with a well-preserved 12th-century Jewish ritual bath (mikveh) — one of the most complete medieval towns in Catalonia.

How to get there

Train: Rodalies R11 from Barcelona to Girona, then a local Teisa bus on to Besalú.

Change needed — swap from train to bus at Girona's bus station, next to the train station (about 25 extra minutes).

Bus: Direct Teisa coach from Barcelona (Gran Via, 658), about 2 hours.

No change needed on the direct bus route.

Gaudí & UNESCO

Colònia Güell

Gaudí's unfinished crypt inside a utopian 19th-century workers' village — the closest UNESCO World Heritage Gaudí site to Barcelona, and the building where he first tested the tilted columns later used at the Sagrada Família.

How to get there

Train: Direct FGC lines S4, S8 or S33 from Plaça Espanya to Colònia Güell station.

No change needed — direct service, about 20–25 minutes.

Bus: Not recommended — the direct FGC train is by far the easiest route.

Outlet shopping

La Roca Village

An open-air designer outlet village with discounted boutiques from international fashion and homeware brands — a popular half-day trip for shoppers.

How to get there

Bus: Direct Sagalés bus, or the dedicated Shopping Express coach, from Barcelona Estació del Nord.

No change needed — about 35–40 minutes direct.

Train: Rodalies R2N to Granollers or Cardedeu, then a short local bus or taxi to the village.

Change needed — the direct bus above is simpler.

Summer Visits

Coastal towns that come alive from late spring through early autumn — beaches, sea breeze and long evenings by the water.

Beach town

Sitges

A polished seaside town with white-washed streets, a lively promenade, boutique shopping and a run of Blue Flag beaches right in the centre. Also known for its February Carnival, but summer is peak beach season.

How to get there

Train: Direct Rodalies R2 Sud from Barcelona-Sants or Passeig de Gràcia.

No change needed — about 35–40 minutes, trains run every 10–20 minutes.

Bus: Direct Monbus coach from Barcelona (Ronda Universitat), about 40 minutes.

Costa Brava

Tossa de Mar

The only fortified medieval old town left on the Catalan coast, its walls looking straight down onto the bay. One of the prettiest stops on the Costa Brava, with several beaches and coves within walking distance.

How to get there

Bus: Direct Moventis coach from Barcelona Estació del Nord, about 1h20.

No change needed — this is the recommended route (Tossa de Mar has no train station).

Train: Rodalies R1 to Blanes, then a local Pujol bus on to Tossa de Mar.

Change needed — adds about 30 minutes; the direct bus above is simpler.

Popular beaches in Tossa de Mar
  • Platja Gran — the main town beach, widest and busiest, right below the old town walls.
  • Platja de la Mar Menuda — calmer water, the best spot for snorkelling and diving.
  • Platja d'es Codolar — a tiny, scenic cove tucked directly beneath the castle walls.
  • Cala Pola — a quieter pine-backed cove a short walk along the coastal path.
  • Cala Giverola — family-friendly, shallow water, about 5 km outside town.

Winter Visits

Mountain escapes that come into their own once the snow arrives.

Pyrenees & ski resort

Vall de Núria

A mountain sanctuary and small ski resort at 2,000 metres in the Catalan Pyrenees, reachable only by rack railway. Skiing and snow activities in winter; the same rack railway also makes it a scenic summer hiking base.

How to get there

Train: Rodalies R3 from Barcelona Plaça Catalunya or Sants to Ribes de Freser (about 2 hours), then the Núria Cremallera rack railway up the mountain.

Change needed — swap from the regional train to the rack railway at Ribes de Freser.

Bus: No direct public bus; the train + rack railway route above is the standard way in.
